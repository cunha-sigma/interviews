# Interview Ops

This project runs interview loops for multiple concurrent roles, staffed via Sigma Software. Each role is self-contained under `roles/<role-slug>/` — its own job description, interview script, Q&A bank with answer keys, candidate pool, and generated reviews. Nothing about one role's content should leak into another's, or into this file beyond a short pointer.

## How a role folder is organized

Each `roles/<role-slug>/` contains:

- `role_description.md` — cleaned job description for that role (named `job_description.md` in `roles/REF4176G (Casual Precision)/` — same purpose, was already client-clean on arrival so wasn't renamed)
- `interview_plan.md` — the interviewer playbook for that role: screening areas, Q&A bank with answer keys, task/live-exercise/system-design script, weighted scorecard, and hire/borderline/no-hire decision guidance. **Source of truth for how to run and grade interviews for that role.**
- `candidates/review_template.md` — post-interview write-up template. **Structure varies per role/client** — some are the generic 5-section 1–5-star format, `python-fde-tabs/` has a client-specific 17-competency version, `REF4176G (Casual Precision)/` uses the client's own bar-scale rubric (English level, Interpersonal, Analytical, Self-Organization, Candidate's Level) transcribed from their own criteria doc. Always read the specific role's copy rather than assuming the shape. No hire/no-hire field of its own in any version — that logic lives only in that role's `interview_plan.md`.
- `candidates/_interview_notes_template.md` — live note-taking template used **during** the interview, mirroring that role's `interview_plan.md` segments. Copy/instantiate per candidate as `candidates/<name>/interview_notes.md`.
- `candidates/<name>/interview_notes.md` — live notes for a specific candidate, filled in during their interview.
- `candidates/<name>/*.pdf` — candidate CVs. `roles/REF4176G (Casual Precision)/` additionally has a parsed `candidates/<name>/cv.md` per candidate (structured transcription, easier to scan than the PDF).
- `candidates/<name>/review.md` — generated by the `evaluate-candidate` skill once notes are filled.

`roles/traffic-shaping-ml/` additionally has `interview_structure.md` (original raw timeline outline) and `scratchpad.md` (detailed project-context research notes). `roles/python-fde-tabs/` additionally has `raw_role_brief.md` (the verbatim hiring brief `role_description.md` was cleaned up from). `roles/REF4176G (Casual Precision)/` additionally has an empty `raw_role_brief.md` (no separate raw source — `job_description.md` arrived already clean), its own `Interview Criteria and Questions.pdf` (source for `candidates/review_template.md`), and `ml_platform_concept_arch_wbs.md` — the client's real concept/architecture doc for the system this hire will work on, used as an **interviewer-only answer key** for `interview_plan.md`'s Q&A and system-design task. Never summarize or leak its contents to a candidate directly — the point is to see what they design themselves. `roles/REF4171X (33 Across)/` similarly has no separate raw brief (`job_description.md` arrived clean) and its own `Interview Criteria and Questions.pdf`; unlike REF4176G it has no separate architecture doc, but the job description's own responsibilities list is detailed enough to serve as the system-design task's answer key directly. `roles/REF4184W (Human Security)/` is the one exception where `raw_role_brief.md` isn't empty — it's a genuinely separate, more detailed internal project brief (transcribed from its own PDF) that arrived alongside `job_description.md` rather than being the raw source it was cleaned from; it also has its own `Interview Criteria and Questions.pdf` (a generic bar-scale template reused from another role, per `candidates/review_template.md`'s note).

## Standing conventions when building a new role

Apply these automatically for every new `roles/<role-slug>/` going forward, not just when reminded:

1. **Always parse PDFs to markdown.** Every candidate CV gets a parsed `candidates/<name>/cv.md` alongside the original PDF — structured, easy to scan, ending with a short "Notable for this role" fit/gap read. Any other role-specific PDF (e.g. a client's interview-criteria doc) gets transcribed into whatever markdown file it feeds (typically `candidates/review_template.md`) rather than left as a PDF-only source.
2. **Always add a short candidate résumé summary to the notes file.** `candidates/_interview_notes_template.md` (and each instantiated `interview_notes.md`) includes a pre-filled resume-summary block near the top, built from the parsed CV before the interview happens — so the interviewer has it in view live, without flipping to `cv.md` separately.
3. **Align the Q&A bank and tasks to the job description's actual requirements** — map every technical section back to something specifically named in that role's `role_description.md`/`job_description.md` (required skills, responsibilities, "strongly preferred" items), not a generic template borrowed from a different role's domain.
4. **Align the tasks/questions with the Interview Criteria doc too**, not just the job description — make sure the interview actually produces usable signal for every category the client's `review_template.md` scores (e.g. don't let Self-Organization or Interpersonal skills be entirely inferred after the fact; include at least one behavioral question deliberately aimed at each criteria category that technical Q&A won't naturally surface).

## Writing conventions (apply everywhere in this project — plans, reviews, replies, this file)

- **Never invent concepts or terms.** Every technical term used (in a Q&A answer key, a review, anything) must be grounded in real usage — either standard industry terminology or something the client's own documents actually say. If unsure a term is real/standard, don't use it.
- **Avoid unnecessary acronyms, and never assume one is known.** The first time an acronym appears in a document, spell out what it means (e.g. "IaC (infrastructure as code)"), even for acronyms that feel obvious from context. Prefer plain descriptions over acronyms generally when a short phrase reads just as easily. Universally standard product/protocol names (AWS, SQL, API, JSON) don't need this treatment — the target is descriptive-phrase shorthand like "JD" for "job description," not brand/protocol names.
- **Anything read verbatim to a live candidate — questions AND role-context scripts — gets asked/said directly, not like a riddle.** This covers every `Q:` in an `interview_plan.md`/`_interview_notes_template.md`, and just as much the Segment 2 "what to tell the candidate" role-context script, which has the same failure mode: it's easy to lift domain jargon straight from a job description (e.g. "supply-side," "over-index," "look-alike audiences from small seed lists") without noticing it needs translating for someone outside that specific industry. Everything the candidate hears must be self-contained and clear enough to understand with zero decoding required. This is not a reading-comprehension test — describe the actual scenario or concept plainly, in concrete terms. Specifically: never quote a jargon phrase (e.g. `"no manual notebook-to-production step"`) and ask the candidate to interpret what it means — state the real situation behind it directly (e.g. "today someone manually exports a model from a notebook and uploads it — what would you build so that's automated, and why does it matter?"). If a question references something (a snippet, a prior answer, a specific scenario), the question text itself must supply that context, not assume it.
- **Answer keys must make sense on their own, not just contain the right keywords.** The interviewer reads the `A:` to understand and evaluate what the candidate actually said — not expecting a verbatim match, but needing enough of a real explanation to judge whether the candidate's own answer was actually correct. A terse list of buzzwords ("upsert, idempotency key, partition by hour") isn't enough — write the reasoning out as a real, complete explanation the interviewer can follow and compare against, the same bar as the question text itself.
- **Don't gate a question on recalling an obscure term's name, even a real one.** A term being grounded/real (per the first rule above) doesn't mean it's safe to lead a question with — some real, client-used terms (e.g. "calibration decay") aren't widely known by name even among engineers who understand the underlying concept perfectly well. Describe the actual phenomenon/scenario in plain terms first, and test whether the candidate can reason about it — only mention the term itself as an interviewer-side aside for reference, not as something the candidate needs to produce or recognize.
- **Use simple, plain words in every question — no idioms, no unexplained jargon.** This includes casual/idiomatic phrasing (e.g. "juggling several things at once" instead of just saying it plainly) as well as domain jargon dropped in without explanation (e.g. "runbooks" — say "written documentation for how to operate the system" instead). If a plainer word or phrase says the same thing, use it. This applies to every question in the bank, not just the technical ones — behavioral/scenario-setup questions need the same treatment.

## Skill: `.claude/skills/evaluate-candidate/`

Role-aware: given a candidate name, it scans `roles/*/candidates/<name>/` to find which role they belong to, then uses that role's `interview_plan.md` and `review_template.md` to grade. Invoke by asking to evaluate/grade/summarize a candidate. Flags gaps instead of inventing scores when notes are thin.

**Standing rule, baked into the skill:** generated `review.md` files are read by people outside this project. They must never reference this project's internal structure or methodology (file names, "the scorecard," segment numbers, weights, etc.) — plain, direct feedback on the candidate's skills only.
