# Candidate Review — Mateus Matos Rizzi

## English level

- Rating: 2 / 5 bars (Pre-Intermediate)
- Feedback: Reads and understands English well, but speaking was difficult. He mispronounced common professional terms badly enough that the interviewer had to repeatedly ask him to repeat himself, confirm understanding out loud, and at times offer hints in Portuguese to keep the conversation moving. This was a real limiting factor in how much depth could come through in his answers.

## Interpersonal skills

- Rating: 3 / 5 bars (Good)
- Feedback:
  - **Receptiveness to feedback:** Took coaching on how to present to the client reasonably well, though stayed fairly passive when asked how he'd handle a manager who disagreed with his technical judgment, essentially defaulting to doing what he's told rather than pushing back constructively.
  - **Collaboration:** Described working on a small team with real constraints (no backend expertise, a tight deadline), and spoke about the team's prioritization process in a way that suggested real involvement in it.
  - **Attitude to work:** Cooperative throughout and open to being pushed for more detail, even when the answers didn't fully hold up under scrutiny.
  - **Communication:** Despite the language difficulty, was able to walk through a business problem and its context clearly at a high level. The breakdown was more in technical depth than in overall structure.

## Analytical skills

- Rating: 2 / 5 bars (Basic)
- Feedback:
  - **Ability to collect and analyze information:** Could describe the overall shape of a real project (an email-based invoice extraction system using AI agents) reasonably well, but needed heavy prompting to get past a surface-level summary into real detail.
  - **Problem-solving:** The team's decision to scope down to the simplest working solution given a tight deadline and limited backend expertise was a reasonable, pragmatic call. However, when asked how a quality score was calibrated and how a pass/fail threshold was chosen, the answers didn't hold together well — no mention of standard concepts like precision or recall, and the explanation for how the threshold was picked didn't make technical sense.
  - **Decision making:** The one clearly reasoned decision he described (picking this project over other options based on team capacity and expected value) was handled by the team as a whole rather than described as his own individual call, so it's hard to credit this specifically to him.

## Self-Organization

- Rating: 3 / 5 bars (Good)
- Feedback: Not directly tested in this conversation. Based on his overall career background — 8+ years across increasingly senior data engineering roles, including leading teams and owning platform work — there's reasonable indirect evidence of self-direction and follow-through, though nothing specific from this interview to point to.

## Candidate's Level

- Rating: 3 / 5 bars (Middle)
- Feedback: He has real, broad engineering experience and is clearly competent in his usual domain of data platform engineering. But on the specific things this role depends on most — going several layers deep into his own past work under follow-up, and reasoning rigorously about an ambiguous problem — he fell short, needing heavy prompting to get past a summary and giving an explanation for a key technical decision that didn't fully add up. That combination keeps this below a Senior rating for this specific role, regardless of his seniority elsewhere.

---

## Recommendation

No hire. His background is solid and he's clearly an experienced, capable engineer in his usual line of work, but this conversation didn't show the specific things this role needs most: the ability to go deep into his own past work when pushed, and rigorous, well-justified reasoning under ambiguity. When asked how a quality threshold was chosen for a system he built, the explanation didn't hold together, and that was after already needing significant prompting to get into real detail on the project at all. English communication was also a real limiting factor — understanding was fine, but speaking was difficult enough that the conversation needed frequent pauses to confirm what he meant, and this role includes direct client communication. It's possible a conversation in his native language would have surfaced more technical depth, but since the role requires working directly with an English-speaking client, that gap remains relevant either way.
